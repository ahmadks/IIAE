The IDICOC protocol enforces a rigid, seven-stage sequence that ensures no data can be

1. Stage 1 – Signal Capture: The raw candidate generative state ($y$) and its

2. Stage 2 – Structural Noise Detection ($X$): Initial screening for topological
anomalies and structural inconsistencies against the current Property Graph.
Structural Noise Detection (X) evaluates deviations using substrate‑appropriate
metrics — including tensor deltas, signal derivatives, spike‑timing variance, or
equivalent representations — each mapped into a unified dissonance space for
deterministic comparison. This ensures substrate‑agnostic detection while
maintaining bounded, deterministic behavior across heterogeneous systems.
The mapping into the unified dissonance space is defined relative to the

3. Stage 3 – Deviation Quantification ($Y$): Execution of the DQE to calculate the
Dissonance Coefficient ($D
_
s$). This stage formalizes the "magnitude of the error.

4. Stage 4 – Resolution: Selection of the deterministic corrective strategy (e.g.,
Invariant Projection, Axiom Conflict Resolution, or Token Re-sampling).
5. Stage 5 – Invariant Correction: Application of the selected correction to produce the
Corrected State ($y^*$).
6. Stage 6 – Cryptographic Sealing: The state transition $\{y \to y^*\}$ is hashed using

intent.
7. Stage 7 – Forensic Audit Receipt: Generation of a signed receipt containing the

Each stage produces a State Artefact that serves as the input for the next, ensuring a
contiguous and unbreakable chain of custody.

5.9.7 Computational Complexity Considerations
The computational complexity of the invention is governed by the properties of the invariance
manifold and the bounded projection operators applied to state transitions. The manifold
projection operator is Lipschitz‑bounded, ensuring deterministic upper bounds on correction
cost. This guarantees that the computational overhead introduced by the DQE and MAII‑ISG
modules remains predictable and stable across substrates.
The Property Graph used for structural evaluation is incrementally updated, enabling
amortized constant‑time updates per state transition in typical deployments. This ensures
scalability in long‑context workloads, high‑frequency signal processing, and distributed
systems with large numbers of interacting agents.
The architecture is compatible with quantized, low‑precision, and edge‑optimized compute
pipelines. In such environments, projection operators can be implemented using
reduced‑precision arithmetic without compromising invariance guarantees. This enables
deployment on constrained hardware while maintaining deterministic structural coherence.
Overall, the invention introduces bounded and substrate‑agnostic computational overhead,
ensuring that the framework remains scalable across large models, high‑throughput compute
systems, and real‑time communication environments.

5.13 Substrate-Specific Considerations
The present invention is designed to operate across heterogeneous computational
substrates. While the core architecture—comprising the MAII‑ISG, DSE, CMC, DQE, and
CTM modules—remains invariant, the manner in which invariance constraints are evaluated
and enforced varies according to the physical and computational characteristics of the
substrate. The following considerations illustrate how the framework adapts to different
classes of systems without requiring substrate‑specific redesign.
5.13.1 Digital Compute Substrates (CPU/GPU/NPU/ASIC)
Digital systems operate over discrete state transitions. In such substrates, the MAII‑ISG and
DQE modules evaluate instruction‑level or tensor‑level deltas and apply bounded projection
operators to ensure that intermediate states remain within the admissible invariance
manifold. This enables deterministic correction of drift, noise accumulation, and structural
inconsistencies in high‑throughput compute pipelines.
5.13.2 Analog Circuits
Analog substrates exhibit continuous signal trajectories rather than discrete transitions. In
this context, invariance is enforced by constraining the evolution of analog signals using
threshold‑based dissonance bounds. Bounded correction operators adjust the trajectory of
the signal to maintain compliance with the manifold, ensuring stability even under noise,
distortion, or component variability.
5.13.3 Neuromorphic Systems
Neuromorphic substrates encode information through spike‑timing patterns. The invention
applies invariance constraints to these spike trains, ensuring that firing sequences remain
within a predefined dissonance envelope. This prevents pathological drift in timing‑based
representations and maintains structural coherence across spiking networks.
5.13.4 RF and Telecommunication Systems
In RF and telecom systems, the DQE module operates on demodulated symbol streams.
Noisy or distorted symbol trajectories are projected into the admissible manifold prior to
decoding, ensuring deterministic correction of channel‑induced perturbations. This enhances
robustness in wireless communication, sensor networks, and distributed control systems.
5.13.5 Distributed and Multi‑Agent Systems
Distributed systems rely on inter‑node message passing. The invention applies manifold
projection to inter‑agent messages to ensure structural consistency across nodes. This
prevents divergence of world models, eliminates inconsistent state propagation, and ensures
deterministic behavior across multi‑agent environments.
